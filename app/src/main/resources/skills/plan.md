# Plan

## Objective

Say what you are going to do before you do it, and do none of it until the
person says yes. The point is the moment before the work: a plan is cheap to
argue with and finished work is not.

## Steps

1. **Understand what is being asked.** Read what is there. Where something is
   genuinely ambiguous and the answer changes the plan, ask - one question, in
   the plan itself, not a conversation of them.
2. **Look, but do not touch.** Reading is allowed and encouraged: read the
   files, the issue, the thread, the logs, whatever tells you what the work
   really is. Nothing that changes anything - no writes, no messages sent, no
   issues filed, no runs started, no state altered anywhere.
3. **Propose the plan.** Numbered steps, in the order you would do them. For
   each step: what you will do, and what it touches - the file, the channel,
   the component. Short lines; this is read, not admired.
4. **Say what is uncertain.** What you assumed, what you could not check, and
   which step would change if the assumption is wrong.
5. **Say what it costs.** What is irreversible, what somebody else will notice
   - a message that goes out, a record that changes, a run that starts - and
   roughly how long it is.
6. **Offer the alternative** where there is a real one: the smaller version,
   the different approach, and why you did not choose it.
7. **Stop and ask.** End with the question plainly: approve this, change it, or
   drop it. Then say nothing further until the person answers.

## What approval is

Only a person saying yes to this plan. Not silence, not "sounds good so far"
about something else, not your own reading that the plan is obviously right. A
step nobody approved is a step not to take.

Where the answer is a change, do not argue it into the plan: rewrite the plan
with the change in it and ask again. Where the answer is a question, answer it
and re-offer the same plan. Where it is no, stop - do not offer a smaller
version of the same thing unless you are asked for one.

## Once approved

Do what the plan says, in the order it says. Where the work turns out different
from the plan - a step that cannot be done as described, something found along
the way that changes the rest - stop there, say what you found, and propose the
amended plan. Approval was for the plan that was read, not for whatever the
work turns into.

Finish by saying what was actually done against what was approved, including
anything left out.
