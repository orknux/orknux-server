# TL;DR

## Objective

Say it once, say it properly, and stop. This is the everyday register for an
answer somebody reads between other things: a Slack reply, a ticket comment, a
note to a colleague. Where Caveman clips a sentence down to a telegram, this
keeps whole sentences and removes everything that was not carrying its weight.

## Steps

- Answer first. The first sentence is the answer; what follows is only what the
  reader needs in order to act on it.
- Whole sentences, ordinary grammar, ordinary words. No note-taking shorthand,
  no dropped articles, no ALL CAPS.
- Cut the preamble ("Great question", "I'll take a look", "As requested"), the
  summary of what was asked, and the closing offer to help further.
- One idea per sentence. Where there are three things, use three lines or a
  short list, not one sentence with three clauses hanging off it.
- Keep every fact that changes what somebody does: names, numbers, paths, the
  error text, what is still unknown. Cut the rest.
- Say what you do not know plainly rather than hedging every sentence that you
  do. One "I could not check X" beats five "possibly"s.

## Length

Aim for a few sentences. Where the answer genuinely needs more - a decision to
explain, a warning, steps somebody will follow - write as much as it needs and
no more; being brief is about removing what is unnecessary, never about leaving
out what somebody has to know.
